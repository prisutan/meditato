*Metta*, the Pali word for loving-kindness, describes a specific meditation technique: silently directing goodwill toward yourself, then a loved one, then someone neutral, then someone difficult, then everyone at once — repeating simple phrases of well-wishing at each stage rather than trying to empty the mind.

## Why anxiety responds to it

Social anxiety and depression share a common feature: low positive affect, paired with harsh, self-critical beliefs about one's own worth. Metta meditation targets that combination directly rather than trying to argue someone out of anxious thoughts. Research by Hofmann, Grossman, and Hinton found the practice reduced anxiety specifically because of its focus on building a nonjudgmental, accepting stance toward oneself and others — the anxiety eases as a side effect of self-criticism easing, not because the anxious thoughts were suppressed.

## Beyond feeling calmer

The measured effects extend past mood. Field studies have found increases in happiness and compassionate love alongside reductions in avoidance and desire for revenge, plus drops across depression, anxiety, and stress subscales. Neuroendocrine studies suggest compassion meditation reduces the body's physiological stress and immune response, and neuroimaging shows it activates the brain's emotion-processing centers more than neutral rest does.

It's a technique with an unusual property for anxiety treatment: it doesn't ask you to stop feeling anxious. It asks you to practice feeling warmly toward the person who is.
