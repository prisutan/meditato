*Nadi shodhana* translates roughly as "subtle energy clearing breath" — a name that sounds mystical until you see what it's actually describing: using the fingers to gently alternate airflow between the left and right nostril, one round at a time.

## Why alternating matters

The traditional claim is that breathing predominantly through the right nostril activates a more analytical, logical mode of mind, while the left favors something more creative and intuitive — and that deliberately alternating between them brings the two into balance. Modern research doesn't fully validate that specific framing, but it does find real physiological effects: over a month of practice, participants showed measurably better oxygen exchange, and regular practice has been linked to reduced systolic and diastolic blood pressure and improved heart rate variability in people with hypertension.

## What the stress data shows

A twelve-week study found lower self-reported stress in male participants after regular practice, and separate research found the technique helped reduce stress specifically in pregnant survivors of intimate partner violence — a population for whom simple, private, equipment-free tools matter. A 2013 randomized study also found measurable cognitive improvement after just twelve minutes of daily practice over four weeks.

It's a technique that asks for almost nothing — no cushion, no app, no quiet room — which may be exactly why it shows up so often in the research on accessible, low-barrier stress interventions.
