Wim Hof, a Dutch extreme athlete, built his method on a combination most meditation traditions would consider strange: rounds of deliberate hyperventilation followed by long breath-holds, paired with progressively colder exposure — cold showers first, ice baths eventually.

## What breath alone does

Isolating the breathing component from the cold exposure turns out to matter. In one study of 48 healthy adults, the group practicing Wim Hof breathing with breath retention showed significantly lower inflammatory markers and higher levels of the anti-inflammatory protein IL-10 — cold exposure by itself did not produce the same effect. A separate trial found the breathing exercises reduced pain perception for at least four hours afterward, and a combined breathwork-and-cold group reported measurable gains in energy, mental clarity, and stress tolerance.

## The claim neuroscience took seriously

The method's most striking result came from brain imaging: scans taken while Hof practiced his breathing technique showed activation in a brain region tied to pain regulation — a region scientists had assumed sat outside conscious control. It was, as far as the researchers were concerned, the first neuroimaging evidence that a person could deliberately influence their own autonomic nervous system through breath alone.

The caveats are real — most trials run only a few weeks, so long-term effects remain unclear. But for a technique built almost entirely on breathing and cold water, the early evidence is more specific than "it's good for you."
