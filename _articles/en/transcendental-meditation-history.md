Maharishi Mahesh Yogi studied under his own teacher in the Himalayas for thirteen years before ever leaving India. When he did leave, in 1959, he brought a simplified meditation technique to a Western audience that turned out to be primed for it.

## From curiosity to movement

Transcendental Meditation grew slowly through the early 1960s, then accelerated sharply when the Beatles and a wave of other celebrities publicly adopted it — turning a specific, silent-mantra technique into a cultural phenomenon among Baby Boomers disillusioned with war and social upheaval. The momentum was substantial enough that, in 1973, followers established Maharishi International University in Fairfield, Iowa — moving roughly 2,000 people to a small town to build a school system running from pre-kindergarten through doctoral programs, all organized around the technique.

## What TM did that most traditions didn't

What sets TM apart from most other meditation lineages covered here isn't the technique itself — a simple, mantra-based practice, taught individually rather than in groups — but its early and sustained commitment to being studied. More than 380 peer-reviewed studies now cover TM's effects, reporting improvements in brain function, creativity, and academic performance, alongside better sleep and reduced anxiety and stress.

That volume of research is itself part of TM's legacy: it helped establish meditation as something a Western university could legitimately study in a lab, not only something to write about anthropologically.
