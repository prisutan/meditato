Most meditation advice assumes more is better: meditate daily, and the benefits compound. A 2026 study published in *Scientific Reports* complicates that assumption in a specific, testable way.

## The counterintuitive finding

Researchers tracking loving-kindness and compassion meditation for anxiety found that practicing two to four days a week produced *clearer* benefits than practicing every single day. The study didn't just report the effect — it identified two mechanisms behind it: participants built more self-compassion, and they showed reduced *cognitive fusion*, the tendency to treat an anxious thought as an objective fact rather than a passing mental event.

## Why less frequent practice might work better

One plausible explanation is that daily practice, done under obligation, can start to feel like another task to perform correctly — undermining exactly the nonjudgmental stance the technique depends on. Practiced a few times a week instead, each session may arrive with more genuine attention and less performance pressure.

This fits a separate 2025 finding from Mount Sinai researchers, who showed meditation's structural brain effects reach beyond the amygdala and cortex into deeper regions tied to memory and emotional regulation — suggesting the benefit isn't purely about daily repetition wearing a groove into the brain, but about the quality of attention brought to each session.

For anyone who has abandoned a daily meditation habit out of guilt, this is closer to permission than to a new obligation.
