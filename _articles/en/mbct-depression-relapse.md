Depression's cruelest feature, for many people, isn't a single episode — it's the recurrence. Researchers Zindel Segal, Mark Williams, and John Teasdale built Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy specifically to interrupt that pattern, targeting the cognitive habits that make someone vulnerable to relapse rather than treating an active depressive episode itself.

## How it's built

MBCT is an eight-week group program that grafts cognitive behavioral therapy techniques onto Jon Kabat-Zinn's MBSR framework. The mindfulness training teaches participants to notice, and disengage from, the repetitive negative thinking that tends to precede a depressive slide — catching the pattern early rather than trying to argue with it once it has taken hold.

## What the trials found

Two randomized controlled trials found that people in remission from three or more prior depressive episodes who completed MBCT relapsed at roughly half the rate of those who didn't — a meta-analysis put the reduction at 43% relative to standard treatment. A separate trial found MBCT held up over a 15-month follow-up as well as ongoing antidepressant maintenance therapy did, for the specific population of people with a recurrent depression history.

It's a rare case in contemplative research: a clearly defined clinical population, a manualized protocol, and a hard, measurable outcome — relapsed or didn't — rather than a self-reported mood score.
