Adolescent anxiety doesn't respond to every intervention adults find helpful, which makes the growing body of school-based meditation research worth reading carefully rather than assuming it simply scales down from adult programs.

## What the controlled studies show

A randomized controlled trial across middle schools found that yoga and mindfulness instruction reduced symptoms of anxiety and depression in young adolescents in a racially diverse urban setting — not a small, self-selected group of already-motivated teens, but a pragmatic, community-based rollout. A separate 10-week school-based mindfulness program produced measurable drops in both depression and anxiety symptoms among the students who completed it. One study using Transcendental Meditation specifically with high-school students found a significant anxiety reduction paired with increased resilience.

## The retreat format works too — differently

Intensive, week-long meditation retreats for adolescents appear to produce results comparable to several weeks of shorter daily practice, with participants in Inward Bound Mindfulness Education (iBme) retreats reporting improved emotional well-being and reduced depression afterward.

## The caveat researchers keep repeating

Experts studying these programs consistently warn against handing a mindfulness curriculum to any available adult and calling it done. The results cluster around instructors with a genuine, sustained personal practice — not around the specific curriculum used. Mindfulness taught as rote behavior modification appears to underperform mindfulness taught by someone who actually does it.
