The standard advice is "every day, without exception," repeated so often it's rarely questioned. A 2026 study gives beginners a reason to question it.

## What the study actually found

Researchers studying loving-kindness and compassion meditation for anxiety compared different practice frequencies and found that two to four sessions a week produced clearer benefits than practicing daily. The likely mechanism: daily practice done out of obligation can start to feel like a task to get through correctly, which works against the nonjudgmental attention the technique depends on in the first place.

## What this means if you're just starting

For a beginner intimidated by "you have to do this every single day," this is closer to good news than a loophole. A realistic three-times-a-week habit that actually sticks will likely outperform an ambitious daily plan that collapses after two weeks of guilt. The goal isn't maximum frequency — it's practice consistent enough, and unpressured enough, that each session gets genuine attention instead of being rushed through to check a box.

This doesn't mean daily meditation is wrong for people who already sustain it comfortably. It means the "every single day or you're failing" framing that scares off so many beginners isn't actually what the evidence supports.
