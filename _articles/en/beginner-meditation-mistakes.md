Most people who quit meditation don't quit because the practice failed them. They quit because they were practicing a version of it that was never going to work in the first place.

## The five that come up again and again

**Unrealistic expectations.** Starting meditation like medication — expecting a specific fix by a specific date — sets up disappointment almost by design.

**Trying to stop thinking.** Meditation isn't the absence of thought; it's noticing when the mind has wandered and returning, gently, without treating the wandering itself as failure.

**Forcing posture.** Full lotus, spine rigid, jaw clenched — beginners often equate physical discomfort with seriousness. It mostly just gives the mind something painful to fixate on instead of settling.

**Starting too long.** Sitting down for a full hour on day one is a reliable way to make day two not happen. One to five minutes is enough to start.

**Inconsistency.** A single 40-minute session on Sunday teaches the brain less than five minutes every day — regularity matters more than duration for building the habit in the first place.

## What actually works instead

Anchor to the breath — it's available, free, and impossible to forget at home. Start small enough that skipping it feels harder to justify than doing it. And treat consistency, not intensity, as the metric that matters until the habit is established on its own.
