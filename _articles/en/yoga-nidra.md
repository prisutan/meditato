Ancient Indian texts like the Mandukya Upanishad describe a fourth state of consciousness — distinct from waking, dreaming, or deep sleep — that later teachers came to call yoga nidra: a state you enter deliberately, rather than one you simply fall into.

## From secret transmission to systematic practice

For most of its history, yoga nidra wasn't written down; it passed directly from teacher to student. That changed in the 1960s, when Swami Satyananda Saraswati of the Bihar School of Yoga codified the technique into a repeatable sequence — guided relaxation, breath awareness, and visualization — that anyone could follow from a book. He built it out of *nyasa*, an older tantric practice of systematically placing attention, or a mantra, on specific parts of the body in sequence.

## What the evidence actually supports

The state was first captured on EEG in 1977, and it has since been studied with fMRI and PET imaging in long-term practitioners. But a 2026 systematic review found only five studies rigorous enough to include in a sleep-focused meta-analysis — enough to justify further research, the reviewers concluded, but not enough to call yoga nidra a proven insomnia treatment.

That's a more honest position than most sleep-and-wellness content takes: a practice with real historical depth and a plausible mechanism, still waiting on the data to catch up to the claims made about it.
