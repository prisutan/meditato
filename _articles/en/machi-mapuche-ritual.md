Among the Mapuche people of southern Chile and Argentina, the *machi* holds a role that doesn't map neatly onto "priest," "healer," or "shaman" as those words are usually used in English. A machi is a religious authority responsible for maintaining balance across the entire Mapuche cosmos — and, notably, that role is defined by spiritual calling and identity, not by the sex a person was assigned at birth; women hold it more often today, but gender in the role is understood as fluid rather than fixed.

## Entering trance as a working method

Central Mapuche ceremonies — the healing ritual *machitún* and the larger communal *nguillatún* — involve the machi entering a trance state using the *kultrun*, a sacred drum built to represent the cosmos itself. Through that trance, the machi is understood to commune directly with ancestors and deities, using the altered state as a working tool rather than a mystical side effect.

## What the ceremonies are actually for

Nguillatún ceremonies combine offerings, prayer, and dance, aimed at securing favor from the spirit world and restoring balance in the natural order. In practice, a machi's responsibilities range widely: curing illness, warding off harm, influencing weather, protecting the harvest, mediating social conflict, and interpreting dreams — a single role that, in most other traditions, would be split across several distinct specialists.
